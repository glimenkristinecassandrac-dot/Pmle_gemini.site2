🍓 Core Psychometric Assessment Formulas

Standard Error of Measurement (SEM)

SEM = s × √(1 - rxx)

Measures amount of error variance in observed scores. When rxx = 1.0, SEM = 0.

Spearman-Brown Prophecy

r_SB = (2 × r_half) / (1 + r_half)

Estimates split-half reliability coefficient corrected for total test length.

Kuder-Richardson Formula 20 (KR-20)

KR-20 = [k/(k-1)] × [1 - Σpq / s²]

For dichotomous (right/wrong) items. Compare with Cronbach's Alpha for Likert scales.

WAIS-IV Four Index Standard Scores

Mean = 100, SD = 15; Subtest M = 10, SD = 3

Indices: VCI, PRI, WMI, PSI. FSIQ composites require certified psychometrics.

🧠 Curated High-Yield PMLE Practice Questions

Developmental Psychology • Major Developmental Theories Moderate
1. According to Erik Erikson’s theory of psychosocial development, an adolescent who explores several alternative occupational paths, belief systems, and social roles without yet making a definitive commitment is in which identity status as defined by James Marcia?
A. Identity Diffusion
B. Identity Foreclosure
C. Moratorium (Correct Answer)
D. Identity Achievement
Rationale: James Marcia defined Moratorium as the status of individuals who are in the midst of an active identity crisis and actively exploring various alternatives, commitments, and philosophies, but have not yet made firm commitments.
🍓 Board Tip: Remember Marcia’s 2x2 grid: Crisis (Exploration) vs. Commitment. High Exploration + Low Commitment = Moratorium.
Developmental Psychology • Cognitive Development Board-Level
2. A 7-year-old child observes two identical glasses filled with equal amounts of juice. When the liquid from one glass is poured into a taller, narrower container, the child correctly deduces that both containers still hold the exact same volume of juice because "you can just pour it back and it will be the same." This justification best demonstrates:
A. Centration
B. Reversibility of thought (Correct Answer)
C. Animistic reasoning
D. Transductive logic
Rationale: In Piaget’s Concrete Operational stage (approx. 7–11 years), children master conservation largely due to reversibility—the cognitive understanding that an action or transformation can be mentally undone or reversed.
🍓 Board Tip: Preoperational children fail conservation tests because of Centration (focusing on one dimension, e.g. height) and lack of Reversibility.
Developmental Psychology • Major Developmental Theories Easy
3. Lev Vygotsky argued that cognitive growth occurs through social interaction within the difference between what a learner can achieve independently versus what they can achieve with guidance from a More Knowledgeable Other (MKO). What term did he coin for this range?
A. Epigenetic Principle
B. Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) (Correct Answer)
C. Schema Equilibration
D. Scaffolding Boundary
Rationale: The Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD) is Vygotsky’s term for tasks that are too difficult for a child to master alone but can be learned with the guidance and assistance of adults or more skilled peers. (Note: Jerome Bruner coined the specific term "scaffolding" based on Vygotsky’s work).
🍓 Board Tip: Board alert: Vygotsky = ZPD & MKO; Bruner = Scaffolding. Do not confuse who originated the terms!
Developmental Psychology • Social-Emotional Development Moderate
4. In Mary Ainsworth’s Strange Situation classification, an infant who explores the room freely when the mother is present, shows marked distress upon separation, but upon reunion actively seeks contact and is promptly comforted is classified as having which attachment pattern?
A. Insecure-Avoidant (Type A)
B. Secure Attachment (Type B) (Correct Answer)
C. Insecure-Resistant/Ambivalent (Type C)
D. Disorganized-Disoriented (Type D)
Rationale: Securely attached infants (Type B) use the caregiver as a secure base to explore the environment. They show separation protest but greet the caregiver with positive affect and soothe quickly upon reunion.
🍓 Board Tip: Type A avoids the caregiver on reunion; Type C shows clinginess mixed with anger/resistance; Type D shows freezing or apprehension.
Developmental Psychology • Lifespan Development Difficult
5. A 15-year-old student who gets a tiny blemish on her forehead is convinced that everyone in school is looking exclusively at her and mocking her. According to David Elkind, this cognitive distortion characteristic of adolescent egocentrism is known as the:
A. Personal Fable
B. Imaginary Audience (Correct Answer)
C. Illusion of Invulnerability
D. False Consensus Effect
Rationale: David Elkind identified two components of adolescent egocentrism: the Imaginary Audience (the belief that others are as interested in them as they are in themselves) and the Personal Fable (the belief in one’s unique invulnerability and special destiny).
🍓 Board Tip: Imaginary audience = "Everyone is watching me"; Personal fable = "Nobody understands me / bad things can never happen to me".
Developmental Psychology • Lifespan Development Board-Level
6. According to Robert Peck’s expansion of Erikson’s final stage of Integrity vs. Despair, late adults must resolve which developmental challenge to achieve healthy psychological aging without tying their self-worth exclusively to their career history?
A. Ego Differentiation vs. Work-Role Preoccupation (Correct Answer)
B. Body Transcendence vs. Body Preoccupation
C. Ego Transcendence vs. Ego Preoccupation
D. Generativity vs. Stagnation
Rationale: Robert Peck specified three adaptations in late adulthood: 1) Ego Differentiation vs. Work-Role Preoccupation (redefining worth beyond career), 2) Body Transcendence vs. Body Preoccupation, and 3) Ego Transcendence vs. Ego Preoccupation.
🍓 Board Tip: Peck expanded Erikson’s last two stages into specific psychological tasks. High-yield for geriatric psychology items!
Developmental Psychology • Major Developmental Theories Moderate
7. According to Urie Bronfenbrenner’s Bioecological Systems Theory, the interaction between a child’s parents and their grade school teachers represents which environmental layer?
A. Microsystem
B. Mesosystem (Correct Answer)
C. Exosystem
D. Macrosystem
Rationale: The Mesosystem comprises linkages and processes taking place between two or more major settings containing the developing person (e.g., relations between home and school, parents and peers).

Abnormal Psychology • Depressive & Bipolar Disorders Board-Level
8. Under the DSM-5-TR, which diagnostic criteria definitively separates a diagnosis of Bipolar I Disorder from Bipolar II Disorder?
A. Bipolar I requires at least one Major Depressive Episode, whereas Bipolar II does not.
B. Bipolar I requires the occurrence of at least one Manic Episode, whereas Bipolar II requires a Hypomanic Episode and a Major Depressive Episode without any manic episode. (Correct Answer)
C. Bipolar I is treated with lithium, while Bipolar II is exclusively treated with SSRIs.
D. Bipolar I has psychotic symptoms in every cycle, while Bipolar II cannot feature rapid cycling.
Rationale: By DSM-5-TR definition, the presence of even a single lifetime Manic Episode satisfies the criteria for Bipolar I Disorder (a major depressive episode is not strictly mandatory for Bipolar I, though common). Bipolar II requires at least one Hypomanic episode AND at least one Major Depressive Episode, with NO history of full manic episodes.
🍓 Board Tip: A full manic episode immediately rules OUT Bipolar II and confirms Bipolar I!
Abnormal Psychology • Personality Disorders Moderate
9. A 28-year-old client displays a pervasive pattern of detachment from social relationships and a restricted range of emotional expression in interpersonal settings. He expresses no desire for close friendships, chooses solitary computer programming hobbies, and appears indifferent to praise or criticism. He denies perceptual distortions or paranoid ideation. The most appropriate diagnosis is:
A. Schizotypal Personality Disorder
B. Schizoid Personality Disorder (Correct Answer)
C. Avoidant Personality Disorder
D. Paranoid Personality Disorder
Rationale: Schizoid Personality Disorder (Cluster A) is characterized by detachment from social relationships and restricted emotional expression. Unlike Avoidant Personality (who yearn for connection but fear rejection) or Schizotypal (who have eccentricities and magical thinking), individuals with Schizoid PD genuinely prefer isolation.
🍓 Board Tip: Schizoid = Solitary & indifferent; Schizotypal = "Magical" eccentricities; Avoidant = Anxious, craves connection but fears humiliation.

B. Deviance, Distress, Dysfunction, and Danger (Correct Answer)

Abnormal Psychology • Schizophrenia Spectrum Disorders Moderate
11. In evaluating a patient with Schizophrenia, which of the following is categorized as a negative symptom rather than a positive symptom?
A. Auditory command hallucinations
B. Delusions of reference
C. Avolition and flat affect (Correct Answer)
D. Grossly disorganized speech (derailment)
Rationale: Positive symptoms represent an excess or distortion of normal functions (hallucinations, delusions, disorganized speech). Negative symptoms reflect a reduction or absence of normal functions (avolition, affective flattening, alogia, anhedonia, asociality).
🍓 Board Tip: Remember the 5 A’s of negative symptoms in Schizophrenia: Affective flattening, Avolition, Alogia, Anhedonia, Asociality.
Abnormal Psychology • OCD, Trauma & Dissociative Disorders Difficult
12. A client survived a severe bus accident in EDSA 2 weeks ago. Since then, he experiences intrusive flashbacks, depersonalization, hyperarousal, and emotional numbness. If full criteria are met, which diagnosis is appropriate given the 2-week duration since the traumatic event?
A. Posttraumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)
B. Acute Stress Disorder (ASD) (Correct Answer)
C. Adjustment Disorder with Anxiety
D. Dissociative Amnesia with Fugue
Rationale: Acute Stress Disorder is diagnosed when symptoms develop within 1 month of the traumatic event and persist for at least 3 days up to 1 month. If symptoms continue beyond 1 month, the diagnosis changes to PTSD.
🍓 Board Tip: Timeline rule: 3 days to 1 month = Acute Stress Disorder. More than 1 month = PTSD.
Abnormal Psychology • Philippine Mental Health Act (RA 11036) Board-Level
13. Under Republic Act No. 11036 (The Philippine Mental Health Act), what is the legal standard regarding a service user’s right to informed consent for psychiatric interventions?
A. Informed consent is not required if the client is diagnosed with any psychotic disorder.
B. Informed consent must be obtained from the service user, except in psychiatric emergencies where there is an imminent threat of serious harm to self or others. (Correct Answer)
C. A barangay captain can unilaterally override informed consent at any time.
D. Only parents or legal guardians can provide informed consent regardless of the patient’s age.
Rationale: RA 11036 explicitly protects the rights of service users, affirming that services must be based on voluntary informed consent, with defined exceptions strictly confined to emergency situations where imminent danger to self or others exists as evaluated by qualified professionals.
🍓 Board Tip: PRC Board exams frequently test provisions of RA 11036 and patient rights regarding involuntary treatment and emergency holds.
Industrial/Organizational Psychology • Personnel Recruitment & Selection Systems Board-Level
14. An HR department uses an employment battery test for managerial applicants. 100 male applicants take the test and 60 are hired (selection rate = 60%). 50 female applicants take the test and 24 are hired (selection rate = 48%). Applying the EEOC / 4/5ths (80%) Rule to assess adverse impact:
A. Adverse impact exists because 48% is less than 80% (which is 0.80 * 60% = 48%, so it exactly equals and does not violate the threshold).
B. Adverse impact does not exist because the selection rate for females (48%) is 80% of the selection rate for males (60% * 0.80 = 48%). (Correct Answer)
C. Adverse impact is present because fewer females applied in total numbers.
D. The 4/5ths rule cannot be applied without conducting a concurrent validation study.
Rationale: The 80% or 4/5ths rule states that adverse impact occurs if the selection rate for a protected group is less than 80% (4/5ths) of the rate for the highest group. Here: 80% of 60% is 48%. Because the female selection rate is 48% (which meets the 80% threshold of the majority group rate), adverse impact is not indicated by this guideline.
🍓 Board Tip: Calculate: Minority selection rate / Majority selection rate. 48% / 60% = 0.80 (80%). If it is 80% or above, no adverse impact.
